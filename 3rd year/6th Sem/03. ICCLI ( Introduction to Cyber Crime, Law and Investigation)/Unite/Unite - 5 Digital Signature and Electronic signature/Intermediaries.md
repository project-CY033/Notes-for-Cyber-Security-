## Why Intermediaries should not qualify for non liability under section 79 of IT Act


# Liability of Intermediary

This exemption is **not absolute**, and an Internet Service Provider (ISP) must meet certain conditions:

1. ISP should not have actual knowledge that the material is illegal or infringing.  
2. ISP is not aware of the facts and circumstances of apparent posting; upon actual knowledge, they must remove or disable access to such material.  
3. ISP must not receive a financial benefit directly attributable to the usage of such illegal or infringing content.

## Section 79(2) of the IT Act, 2000

Non-liability shall apply if:

- **(a)** The function of the intermediary is limited to providing access to a communication system over which information made available by third parties is transmitted, temporarily stored, or hosted.  
- **(b)** The intermediary does not:  
  1. Initiate the transmission,  
  2. Select the receiver of the transmission, and  
  3. Select or modify the information contained in the transmission.  
- **(c)** The intermediary observes due diligence while discharging duties under this Act and follows such guidelines as the Central Government may prescribe.


---

# Section 79 of the IT Act

The intent behind inserting **Section 79** is to provide a **safe harbor** for intermediaries, ensuring they are not held liable unreasonably.

- "Liable unreasonably" is a question of facts.

---

## Section 79(2)(a)

> “The function of the intermediary is limited to providing access to a communication system over which information made available by third parties is transmitted or temporarily stored or hosted; or”

- To avail the defense of non-liability under Section 79(2)(a), the intermediary should restrict its functions to transmitting, hosting, and temporarily storing data.  
- However, in certain cases, such as matrimonial sites, this may not always apply.

---

## Section 79(2)(b)

> “The intermediary does not—  
> (i) initiate the transmission,  
> (ii) select the receiver of the transmission, and  
> (iii) select or modify the information contained in the transmission;”

- If the above conditions are complied with, the intermediary could potentially qualify for non-liability.  
- Many **web-hosting services** may still be considered as initiating, selecting receivers, or modifying the intended transmission, which can affect their liability status.

---

## Section 79(2)(c)

> “The intermediary observes due diligence while discharging his duties under this Act and also observes such other guidelines as the Central Government may prescribe in this behalf.”

- Intermediaries are required to observe **due diligence** as prescribed in the **IT (Intermediaries Guidelines) Rules, 2011**.

---

## Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines) Rules, 2018

- **Rule 3(2)(j)** requires that the privacy policy/user agreement must inform users **not** to host, display, upload, modify, publish, transmit, update, or share any information that:
  - Threatens public health or safety.
  - Promotes cigarettes, tobacco products, intoxicants (including alcohol), or Electronic Nicotine Delivery Systems (ENDS) and similar nicotine delivery products, **except** as permitted under the **Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940** and related rules.

# Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines) Rules, 2018

## Rule 3(4) – Monthly User Notification
- Intermediaries must inform their users **at least once a month** that non-compliance with the rules, regulations, user agreements, and privacy policies will:
  - Allow the intermediary to **immediately terminate access or usage rights**.
  - Lead to removal of non-compliant information.
- **Obligation:** Inform users that certain compliances are mandatory.

# Intermediary Guidelines (2011)

These rules may be called the **Information Technology (Intermediaries Guidelines) Rules, 2011**.

- Made in exercise of powers under clause (zg) of subsection (2) of section 87 read with sub-section (2) of section 79 of the **IT Act, 2000**.

---

## Rule 3 – Due Diligence

**Meaning:** Due diligence means taking reasonable measures to avoid committing an offence.

### Rule 3(1)
- The intermediary shall **publish**:
  - Rules and regulations
  - Privacy policy
  - User agreement

### Rule 3(2)
- These regulations shall inform users not to host, display, upload, modify, publish, transmit, update, or share information that:
  1. Belongs to another person without proper authorization.
  2. Is harmful, harassing, defamatory, obscene, or otherwise objectionable.
  3. Harms minors in any way.
  4. Infringes any proprietary rights (patent, trademark, copyright, etc.).
  5. Violates any law in force.
  6. Deceives or misleads the addressee about the origin of such messages.
  7. Impersonates another person.
  8. Contains software viruses.
  9. Threatens the unity, integrity, defence, security, or sovereignty of India.

### Rule 3(3)
- The intermediary shall not knowingly:
  - Host or publish prohibited information.
  - Initiate the transmission.
  - Select the receiver of the transmission.
  - Select or modify the information in the transmission.

### Rule 3(4)
- Preserve information and associated records for **at least 90 days** for investigation purposes.

### Rule 3(5)
- Inform users that non-compliance with the rules, user agreement, or privacy policy may result in **immediate termination of access**.

### Rule 3(6)
- Strictly follow provisions of the **IT Act, 2000** or any other laws in force.

### Rule 3(7)
- Provide information or assistance to government agencies when required by lawful order.

### Rule 3(8)
- Take all reasonable measures to secure computer resources.

### Rule 3(9)
- Report cyber security incidents.

### Rule 3(10)
- Not knowingly deploy, install, or modify technical configurations of computer resources, or be party to such acts.

### Rule 3(11)
- Publish on the website:
  - Name of the **Grievance Officer**.
  - Contact details of the Grievance Officer.
