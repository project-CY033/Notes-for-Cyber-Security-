# SECTION ONE: INTRODUCTION

## 1.1 Common Terminologies

**Electronic governance (e-governance)**:  
The integration of **Information and Communication Technology (ICT)** in all processes with the aim of enhancing government’s ability to address the needs of the general public.  

- Purpose: Simplify processes for government, citizens, and businesses at national, state, and local levels.  
- Means: Use of electronic tools to promote **good governance**.  
- Involves:
  - Implementation of IT in government processes to ensure simple, moral, accountable, and transparent governance.
  - Quick and efficient access, delivery of government services, dissemination of information, and communication.

**Electronic government (e-government)**:  
The use of technological communication devices (such as computers and the Internet) to provide public services to citizens and others in a region or country.  

Also known as: *e-gov, Internet governance, digital government, online government, connected government*.

**Examples of e-government services**:
- E-procurement
- Filing tax returns
- Renewing ID, passport, or driving license
- Applying for extracts from criminal or commercial registers
- Forms and applications for starting a business

## 1.2 Difference Between E-Governance and E-Government

Although the terms are often used interchangeably, they are not the same.

- **E-government**:  
  The use of ICT in public administration, combined with organizational change and new skills, to improve public services, democratic processes, and support to the public.  
  *No provision for governance of ICTs.*

- **E-governance**:  
  The use of technologies that both help to govern and **have to be governed**.  
  Requires more regulation, policy-making capabilities, expertise, and engagement of various social stakeholders.  
  The main goal is to ensure beneficiaries’ service needs are met.

### Major Differences

| Aspect | E-Government | E-Governance |
|--------|--------------|--------------|
| Scope  | System for applying ICT in government operations | Functionality to regulate and guide ICT use in government |
| Nature | One-way protocol (Government → Citizen) | Two-way protocol (Government ↔ Citizen) |
| Role   | A tool to make government operations better | A framework to control, regulate, and improve services provided by e-government |
| Relation | Exists independently as a system | Always accompanies e-government as a governing mechanism |

## 1.3 Benefits of E-Governance

- Reduced corruption  
- High transparency  
- Increased convenience  
- Growth in GDP  
- Direct participation of constituents  
- Reduction in overall cost  
- Expanded reach of government  

**Goal**: Raise the coverage and quality of information and services provided to the public using ICT in an easy, economical, and effective manner.

**Note**: This requires proper arrangement of hardware, software, networking, and re-engineering of processes to ensure better service delivery.

---


## 1.4 Types of Interactions in E-Governance

### A. Government to Government (G2G)  
Exchange of information and services **within** the government.  
- **Horizontal G2G**: Between various government entities.  
- **Vertical G2G**: Between national, state, and local government entities, or within different levels of the same entity.

### B. Government to Citizen (G2C)  
Interaction between the government and the general public.  
- Citizens access a wide variety of public services.  
- Citizens can share their views and grievances on government policies anytime, anywhere.

### C. Government to Business (G2B)  
Facilitates seamless interaction between businesses and the government.  
- Aims to eliminate red tape, save time and costs, and ensure transparency in business-government dealings.

### D. Government to Employees (G2E)  
Covers interactions between the government (as the largest employer) and its employees.  
- ICT makes these interactions faster and more efficient.  
- Improves employee satisfaction by enabling better communication and providing perks and benefits.

---

**Note:**  
E-governance is only possible if the government is ready for it.  
This requires:
- Investment in telecommunication infrastructure  
- Budget allocation and resource planning  
- Ensuring security and monitoring assessments  
- Improving internet connectivity speed  
- Promoting public awareness on e-governance  
- Support from all government departments

**Impact:**  
E-governance improves and supports government tasks by simplifying processes and increasing the quality of services.

---

## 1.5 Limitations of E-Governance

A full-scale government-to-citizen e-governance implementation requires **significant investment** in development and infrastructure.

### Common Barriers:
- Lack of internet access, especially in rural or low-income areas  
- Limited computer skills among citizens  
- Technical requirements (e.g., browser compatibility, plug-ins) that may block access  
- Language barriers  
- Need for an email address to use certain services  
- Privacy concerns and insufficient data protection
